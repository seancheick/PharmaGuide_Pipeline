# Applicability review — 2026-09-03

This is content review, not clinician sign-off or a calibrated scoring benchmark. No AFU/CFU conversion is supported.

## Seed DS-01 — formula-level evidence

Primary full text checked through Europe PMC's fullTextXML API for PMC12845427 and PMC12430871, with PubMed/publisher identity cross-checks.

- PMID **41599868**, DOI **10.3390/nu18020255**, published 2026-01-14: *A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating Multi-Species Synbiotic Supplementation for Bloating, Gas, and Abdominal Discomfort*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41599868/
  - Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled six-week study: 350 baseline participants, 219 evaluable at week six; last-observation-carried-forward for final two timepoints. Self-reported bloating/indigestion in otherwise healthy adults, not a disease-treatment population.
  - Intervention: the 24-strain DS-01 consortium, 53.6 billion AFU plus 400 mg Indian pomegranate extract (>40% polyphenols), ViaCap capsule-in-capsule. One capsule daily for three days, then two daily. The matching target is the maintenance dose, not an invented per-strain dose.
  - Reported digestive symptom/quality-of-life improvements; short duration, substantial attrition, self-report, sponsor funding and employee/advisor conflicts limit certainty. Seed funded; Radicle ran operations. No independent-strain, skin, cardiovascular or micronutrient-synthesis efficacy is established by this formula trial.
- PMID **40944126**, DOI **10.3390/nu17172734**, published 2025-08-23: *Multi-Species Synbiotic Supplementation Enhances Gut Microbial Diversity, Increases Urolithin A and Butyrate Production, and Reduces Inflammation in Healthy Adults: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40944126/
  - Same formula/dose/delivery for 91 days. 32 randomized; 27 completed. Mostly microbiome/metabolite endpoints; butyrate benefit was evaluated in a low-baseline subgroup, and CRP findings include association analyses. Do not restate these as universal clinical disease benefits.
  - Seed funded, with employee/shareholder/advisor conflicts; independent trial operations by KGK. Small supportive mechanistic study, not independent replication of the six-week digestive clinical endpoint.

The full texts name SD150-BE and RD830-FR where the submitted label uses SD-150-BE and SD-RD830-FR. Only these explicit, within-formula code variants are equated, with the corresponding species retained. No prefix-stripping rule may generalize that equivalence to other strains.

Manufacturer identity cross-check: https://seed.com/approach lists the corresponding named strains, including SD-150-BE and SD-RD830-FR. https://help.seed.com/en-US/articles/ds-01-daily-synbiotic-295557 confirms the two-capsule/53.6-billion-AFU daily serving. These manufacturer sources establish product identity and labeling only, not independent efficacy. The submitted four blend amounts (37, 8.05, 3.3 and 5.25 billion AFU) are individually checked alongside their exact strain membership; redistributing the same total between blends is not a match.

## Zinc cold-lozenge applicability

PMIDs **25888289**, **27378206**, **28515951** concern acetate/gluconate lozenges for acute common colds, not zinc picolinate/bisglycinate capsules. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28515951/ describes seven trials / 575 participants at >75 mg/day (reported groups 80–92 and 192–207 mg/day). Evidence must require the studied forms, lozenge delivery and directed daily dose; it is not a claim of preventive benefit or a dosing recommendation. Separate UL and safety assessment still applies. Broad `zinc` alias alone cannot grant therapeutic credit.

## Vitamin A / beta-carotene contextual references

PMIDs **23644932**, **35653117**, **37702300** concern AREDS/AREDS2 combinations or antioxidant/mineral formulations for selected AMD populations. They do not establish independent efficacy of a low-dose retinyl-palmitate multivitamin ingredient. Preserve them as contextual references, but disallow individual-ingredient clinical point credit. Established dietary vitamin-A roles remain nutrition-authority evidence, not a claim that vitamin A lacks a role.

## KSM-66

NIH ODS summarizes short-term ashwagandha trials, including KSM-66, with studied 250/600 mg daily doses and uncertainty beyond short-term use. https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Ashwagandha-HealthProfessional/
PMIDs **23439798**, **32021735** require checking the exact extract/dose and stress/sleep scope. A mass-dominant branded identity alone cannot prove dose applicability; unknown or unconvertible units must not earn a clinically-dosed floor.

## Parent nutrient

NIH ODS explicitly states that Supplement Facts magnesium quantity denotes elemental magnesium, not the source-compound mass. https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/ . FDA ingredient labeling permits a parent nutrient with `(as source)` notation. This does not authorize converting a standalone compound-mass row into an elemental dose.

## Probiotic strain applicability

NIH ODS distinguishes shared mechanisms from species- and strain-specific effects and recommends strain-specific clinical interpretation: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Probiotics-HealthProfessional/ (search-index content checked 2026-09-03; direct page returned HTTP 403). The shared gate does not say unstudied strains are ineffective. It prevents an NCFM-specific clinical record from being credited to another acidophilus strain, or a Seed formula record from being credited to an unrelated plantarum product. Independently establishing species-wide evidence would require its own reviewed scope, not an identity shortcut.

## Certification identity

The exact Thorne Zinc Picolinate 30 mg product (DSLD 291803) was checked in the [official NSF dietary-supplement listings](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/Dietary/Listings.asp) on 2026-09-03. This is the only new credit discovered by the all-program certification audit. A matching flavor, population-incompatible product, or missing named formulation addition does not establish SKU identity; uncertain cases retain a review disposition rather than a factual assertion that a certificate does not exist.

## KSM-66 content verification completed — 2026-09-03

Both identifiers were fetched individually with the repository PubMed client, disk cache disabled; titles, DOIs, abstracts and non-retracted status matched. Full-text XML was retrieved from Europe PMC for [PMC3573577](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC3573577/fullTextXML) and [PMC6979308](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6979308/fullTextXML).

- [PMID 23439798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23439798/), DOI 10.4103/0253-7176.106022: 64 randomized adults with chronic stress; 300 mg twice daily for 60 days. Full text explicitly identifies KSM-66, root-only extract, supplied by Ixoreal. Primary observed outcomes include stress scales and cortisol.
- [PMID 32021735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32021735/), DOI 10.7759/cureus.6466: published 2019-12-25; 60 randomized, 58 completed, eight weeks. Daily groups were 250 mg, 600 mg and placebo. Full text names KSM-66 supplied by Ixoreal. Perceived stress, cortisol and self-rated sleep improved at both doses; anxiety improvement was not significant at 250 mg/day. Small short-term trials and manufacturer-supplied extract limit generalization.

The record's denominator is 124 randomized participants across these trials. The 250–600 mg/day envelope does not mean every outcome was demonstrated at every dose. Form applicability excludes non-KSM-66 powder; dose scoring separately identifies known underdosing and cannot borrow another row's quantity. Unverified registry counts and compound UNIIs were removed rather than promoted as brand-specific evidence.
