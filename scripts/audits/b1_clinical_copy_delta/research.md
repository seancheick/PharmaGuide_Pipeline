# B1 clinical-copy delta — primary-source record

Reviewed: 2026-07-27

Scope: ten active B1 records reopened after the original sign-off, plus two
wording corrections on the separately suppressed B1.1 candidates.

## Verified findings

- **Levothyroxine + iron** — PMID 1443969 describes an *uncontrolled clinical
  trial* in 14 patients with hypothyroidism over 12 weeks. The prior authored
  copy incorrectly called it controlled.
- **Levothyroxine + calcium** — PMID 10838651 is the clinically relevant
  20-patient hypothyroidism cohort (mean TSH 1.6 to 2.7 mIU/L; 20% above the
  normal range). PMID 11716045 is a seven-volunteer acute pharmacokinetic study
  in people without thyroid disease using 1,000 mcg levothyroxine plus 2 g
  calcium. The DailyMed label directs levothyroxine separation from calcium
  carbonate/ferrous sulfate, not ordinary calcium-rich meals.
- **Acid suppression + iron** — PMID 27890768 reports that observed
  iron-deficiency risk decreased after medication discontinuation; it does not
  establish complete reversal.
- **Warfarin + vitamin K** — the current DailyMed label says anticoagulant
  effect generally occurs within 24 hours, peaks at 72–96 hours, and a single
  dose acts for 2–5 days. The prior `weeks` onset was too slow.
- **PPI + magnesium candidate** — PMID 22762246 reports onset from 14 days to
  13 years (median 5.5 years). `variable` is more faithful than `months`, and
  the alert now reconciles earlier cases with the predominance of prolonged
  use.
- **Cholestyramine + fat-soluble vitamins** — the current label directs other
  medicines at least 1 hour before or 4–6 hours after cholestyramine and says
  water-miscible (or parenteral) fat-soluble vitamin forms should be considered
  during long-term therapy.
- **Vitamin A safety** — NIH ODS states that excess preformed vitamin A can
  cause congenital birth defects and advises people who are or might become
  pregnant not to take more than 3,000 mcg RAE (10,000 IU) daily from vitamin A
  supplements. The authored cards use form/dose review rather than implying
  that more vitamin A is universally better.

## Provenance correction

PMID 28906303 appeared in the user-supplied clinical-team feedback attachment,
not in the repository or the B1.1 candidate review. It is an unrelated
rat/alcohol paper and is not used as evidence here. The warfarin onset
correction is independently supported by the authored DailyMed label.
