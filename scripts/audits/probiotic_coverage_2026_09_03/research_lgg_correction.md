# LGG specificity and contradictory secondary copy — 2026-09-03

Bounded review by Codex (AI-assisted source verification, not a new clinician
sign-off). Questions: does the already-cited primary guideline explicitly name
LGG, and does the already-cited Cochrane review establish diarrhea reduction?

Live PubMed pages and the repository's `verify_all_citations_content.fetch_articles`
confirmed both identifiers and titles on 2026-09-03. The verifier truncates abstracts;
the full PubMed abstract was additionally read for conclusions.

1. PMID 26756877, *Probiotics for the Prevention of Antibiotic-Associated
   Diarrhea in Children.* https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26756877/
   ESPGHAN working-group guideline, 2016. Explicitly names **Lactobacillus
   rhamnosus GG** and recommends it for prevention of antibiotic-associated
   diarrhea in children when prevention is considered. Strong recommendation,
   moderate-quality evidence. It does not assess treatment of established AAD.
   The abstract does not establish an adult daily-wellness CFU range. Therefore
   q1 strain-explicit and q3 human-clinical can be recorded YES, but no generic
   dose-applicability reference is created and existing strength is unchanged.
2. PMID 33295643, *Probiotics for treating acute infectious diarrhoea.*
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33295643/ (Cochrane, 2020).
   82 trials, heterogeneous agents and settings. Low-risk trials showed no
   difference in diarrhea lasting at least 48 hours; duration was uncertain.
   The abstract discusses LGG and S. boulardii subgroups but does not justify
   the registry's unconditional secondary-indication label "duration reduction".
   Retain the citation and describe the uncertainty, not an established benefit.
