# Section 7 — diabetes / metabolic medication–nutrient review

Reviewed: 2026-07-26

## Decision record

| Record | Decision | Evidence review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DEP_METFORMIN_FOLATE` | Needs revision; suppressed | The long-term placebo-controlled metformin trial (PMID 20488910) measured a small unadjusted folate decrease, but states that the adjusted analysis found no significant folate effect. It establishes neither folate deficiency nor the displayed consumer supplementation warning. PMID 14535967 similarly reports a short-term serum change but says clinical significance remains to be investigated. |
| `DEP_DIABETESMEDS_MAGNESIUM` | Rejected | The record itself attributes low magnesium to type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, glycosuria, and renal handling. Its only duplicated ODS source contains no medication-specific evidence for sulfonylureas or thiazolidinediones. A disease-associated magnesium finding cannot support a medication depletion alert. |
| `DEP_INSULINS_MAGNESIUM` | Rejected | PMID 26696633 is a review of hypomagnesemia in type 2 diabetes, not evidence that prescribed insulin causes chronic magnesium depletion. The current copy conflates transient intracellular shifts and poor-control glycosuria with an insulin-caused consumer deficiency claim. |

## Source verification

- PMID 20488910, de Jager et al., randomized placebo-controlled trial: metformin vs placebo for 4.3 years in insulin-treated type 2 diabetes; folate was a measured outcome. Its abstract reports a -5% unadjusted folate difference and no significant effect after adjustment for BMI and smoking.
- PMID 14535967, Wulffelé et al., randomized placebo-controlled trial: 16-week metformin add-on trial; reports a serum folate decrease and explicitly says the clinical significance remains to be investigated.
- PMID 26696633, Barbagallo & Dominguez, review: addresses hypomagnesemia in type 2 diabetes; it does not establish a chronic insulin-treatment magnesium depletion effect.

No medication or nutrient identity was newly introduced, so no RxCUI/CUI mutation or interaction-taxonomy rebuild is required.
