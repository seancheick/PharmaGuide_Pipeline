# Native probiotic context curation — 2026-09-04

Bounded primary-source review, AI-assisted. This is not a systematic review,
clinical sign-off, or a dosing recommendation. Existing clinician approval flags
must not be extended to these newly reviewed contexts. Missing information stays
unknown; publication counts do not equal independent trial counts.

## Verification and retrieval

The research pass checked publication identity and methods/outcomes. The parent
independently retrieved BB-12 and HN019 full text from Europe PMC's fullTextXML
API, Bi-07 papers from NCBI's BioC full-text API, and the original LGG article
PDF reproduced by UNAM. API/HTML retrieval failures were not counted as evidence
of absence. PubMed links below identify the original publications.

Final identifier check used the existing NCBI efetch verifier on all **13 unique
PMIDs** in the 14 contexts: 13 retrieved, zero missing; titles matched these
research records. New context citations also enter the existing citation gate.
This identifier/topic check complements the individual methods/outcome reviews
below; it does not turn an automated text match into clinical approval.

### LGG

- [PMID 26756877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26756877/): ESPGHAN guideline
  explicitly supports LGG for **prevention** of antibiotic-associated diarrhea
  in children at risk. It does not establish adult general-use, treatment of
  established diarrhea, or a precise dose from the inspected abstract.
- [PMID 17229242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17229242/): 104 children aged
  6–16 with Rome II abdominal-pain disorders, four-week randomized capsule trial.
  Authors report more pain-free responses, concentrated in the 37-child IBS
  subgroup; no improvement in pain severity and no clear FD/FAP subgroup benefit.
  The small subgroup and wide intervals limit inference.
  **Do not turn this into an exact dose match:** Methods on printed page 178
  and Figure 1 on page 180 describe 3 billion CFU twice daily, whereas Discussion
  on page 183 states 3 billion CFU daily. No per-capsule allocation is inferred.
  Also, Table 2 reports overall treatment-success P=.08 beside an effect interval
  excluding one; preserve that reporting inconsistency instead of resolving it
  in favor of benefit. The source can establish a research context, not an
  unambiguous automated daily-dose reference.
  [Original article PDF, university mirror](https://dsp.facmed.unam.mx/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/U12-Anexo-16.-Gawronska.-A-randomized-double-blind-placebo-controlled-trial-of-Lactobacillus-GG-for-abdominal-pain-disorders-in-children.pdf).

### BB-12

- [PMID 26382580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26382580/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4657032/): 1,248 generally
  healthy adults with low stool frequency and discomfort; separate 1-billion
  and 10-billion CFU once-daily capsule arms for four weeks. These are discrete
  arms, not a supported 1–10 billion efficacy interval.
- Original frequency-responder analysis was not conventionally significant
  overall (OR 1.31, 95% CI .98–1.75); global relief was not improved. A tightened
  **post hoc** responder definition and average frequency analyses were more
  favorable. Similar effects at both doses do not justify a high-CFU advantage.
- Funded by Chr. Hansen with disclosed industry relationships. This is not a
  diagnosed functional-constipation population or broad proof of gut benefit.

### HN019

- [PMID 39356506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39356506/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11447574/): 229 adults,
  Rome III functional constipation, eight weeks; a 2-g sachet once daily.
  Potency measured 7.0 billion CFU at the first visit and 4.69 billion at the
  last. These are **viability measurements of one regimen**, not randomized
  dose arms or a clinically effective interval. Primary complete spontaneous
  bowel movements did not improve over placebo (P=.37). Sponsor funded and
  participated in study planning/analysis/interpretation.
- [PMID 40320938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40320938/): a separate French
  multicenter eight-week functional-constipation RCT also reported no superiority
  for its primary bowel-movement outcome. Dose/formulation were not established
  from the accessible primary abstract and remain unresolved.
- Null constipation outcomes do not prove absence of benefit for every outcome.

### Bi-07

- [PMID 21436726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21436726/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4372813/): 60 adults with
  non-constipation functional bowel disorders; NCFM **plus** Bi-07 for eight
  weeks. Twice-daily pills contain 100 billion total CFU each, in equivalent
  amounts: **200 billion total daily**, with 100 billion of each strain daily.
  That component arithmetic is from the study's explicit equal mixture; it must
  never be used to allocate an unspecified product blend. Both primary global
  relief/satisfaction outcomes were negative; bloating was a secondary signal.
  This is combination evidence, not Bi-07-alone efficacy. Industry support and
  the small heterogeneous pilot population limit interpretation.
- [PMID 36149331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36149331/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9761758/): two acute
  lactose-challenge crossover trials in adults with lactose intolerance.
  Methods give 2.40 trillion CFU for Booster Alpha and 2.34 trillion for Booster
  Omega; the abstract rounds to 2 trillion. These are separate study-specific
  amounts, not a continuous dose window. The powder was taken with 25 g lactose
  in milk (Alpha) or water (Omega), not an ordinary daily capsule regimen.
  The primary breath-hydrogen outcome is a **surrogate**. Symptoms were not
  better than placebo; Omega reported more nausea and two vomiting events in
  the ITT population. Do not promote this to broad symptom-relief evidence.

### Saccharomyces boulardii

- [PMID 26756877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26756877/) also names this
  organism for pediatric antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention. The
  inspected source does not establish a CNCM I-745 code or universal CFU range.
- [PMID 22472744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22472744/): elderly hospitalized
  adults, antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention, twice-daily capsules from
  within 48 hours of antibiotics through seven days after withdrawal; no
  preventive superiority. Exact dose and commercial strain code were not
  verified in the accessible abstract.
- [PMID 41675330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41675330/): 72 severely ill
  patients with non-C. difficile nosocomial diarrhea, five-day adjunctive
  **treatment**, not prevention. No advantage in stool outcomes; no placebo arm
  and blinding limitations. Exact dose and strain code remain unresolved.
- Species-level naming cannot authenticate CNCM I-745. Preserve prevention vs
  treatment and pediatric vs elderly/severely ill populations separately.

### NCFM

- [PMID 28082816](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28082816/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5192275/): 391 adults
  randomized to NCFM or placebo for 12 weeks. Primary IBS-SSS did not improve
  versus placebo; a post hoc moderate/severe-pain subgroup was more favorable.
  The parent checked NCBI BioC methods and results: nominal daily capsule arms
  are 1 and 10 billion CFU, but the reported low-dose viability exceeds
  9.8 billion. Preserve the contradiction; do not create a numeric efficacy
  window or silently correct the paper. Industry supplied the product and
  participated in the study.
- [PMID 19651563](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19651563/): 326 children aged
  3–5, three arms including NCFM alone and NCFM + Bi-07, twice daily for six
  months. The NCFM-only arm reported less fever/cough, but not a significant
  reduction in rhinorrhea incidence. Exact dose/form and prespecified endpoint
  hierarchy are unresolved in the accessible abstract. Combination results
  are not attributed to NCFM alone. Six months is not invented as 180 days.
- The old mouse-study anchor (24717228) remains historical, not evidence that
  no human studies exist. New human records require fresh clinical approval;
  the old approval cannot be inherited by swapping its PMID.

### Bl-04

- [PMID 24268677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24268677/): 465 physically
  active adults; Bl-04 alone at 2 billion CFU/day in a drink, a separate NCFM +
  Bi-07 arm and placebo. Bl-04 respiratory-illness HR .73 (95% CI .55–.95);
  combination result was not significant. Too few GI episodes for inference.
  Duration and endpoint hierarchy are unresolved from the inspected abstract.
- [PMID 28343401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28343401/),
  [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5797652/): 152 volunteers
  randomized before experimental rhinovirus inoculation; 28 pre-challenge plus
  five post-challenge days. Parent checked NCBI BioC methods: daily sachet
  contains a **minimum** 2 billion CFU, not an exact dose or a clinically
  supported open-ended range. Primary nasal CXCL8 is a surrogate; symptom
  scores and infection rate were not improved. Sponsor involvement and baseline
  marker imbalance are limitations. This is not routine digestive efficacy.
- These sources establish human research missing from the current weak anchor,
  not a new clinician sign-off or a verified publication-count claim.

## Implementation consequence

Store the contexts on the existing native registry and assess them through the
existing source-owned formula/strain boundary. Separate source verification,
clinical approval, label-dose comparability, study population/outcomes/regimen,
and effect direction. No context may silently unlock points merely because it
has a PMID, a matching species, a marketing keyword, or a CFU number between two
tested arms. New contexts remain pending clinical approval; the source-review
work is useful and auditable without inventing that approval.
