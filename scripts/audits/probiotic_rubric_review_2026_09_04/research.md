# Primary-source review ledger — 2026-09-04

Bounded literature review, not a systematic review, completed clinical curation,
medical advice or clinician sign-off. No registry entries changed. PubMed records
were checked by identifier **and title/abstract content**, using Europe PMC's
primary-record API where PubMed/PMC page rendering was inaccessible. Missing
full-text details stay missing; an abstract-only check is not a complete trial
quality assessment. Results below are paraphrases, not copied abstracts.

Search concepts included exact strain codes plus randomized trial, indication,
dose and guideline; Seed's exact commercial formula; and newer HN019 constipation
trials. No search was interpreted as proof of absence. Catalog reach is from the
551-product replay, not commercial market share.

## Formula trial families and search limits

The earlier source-backed [formula audit](../probiotic_coverage_2026_09_03/research_formulas.md)
records full-text review for Seed's symptom trial and two mechanistic publications:
[PMID 40944126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40944126/) and
[PMID 41750436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41750436/).
Those mechanistic papers share a protocol; do not count them as two independent
symptom replications or sum overlapping enrollment. This report carries forward
that documented review rather than pretending to have repeated its full-text
extraction today.

The same prior bounded search did not establish an exact Garden of Life or
Fortify 100B formula trial. Their overlapping strain research is not evidence of
the entire commercial formulation. Absence from that search is not demonstrated
absence of research. Current label editions must be matched individually.

Ritual, Culturelle, Jarrow and Solgar are mandatory **product controls**, not
automatically reviewed finished formulas. Exact strain, dose, delivery, additions,
population and indication must agree before any product-specific credit is
upgraded. No sales data were inspected, so “high-market-share” remains unknown.

## Guideline context and design implications

[NIH ODS, Probiotics — Health Professional](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Probiotics-HealthProfessional/)
supports strain/condition-specific interpretation, warns that larger CFU counts
do not necessarily improve effectiveness, and discusses shelf-life counts.
It does not establish our 1B/10B/50B scoring ladder or prove that all mixtures are
inferior. Some combinations have condition-specific supporting evidence.

[AGA's gastrointestinal guideline](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/role-of-probiotics-in-the-management-of-gastrointestinal-disorders/)
recommends probiotics for IBS only in a clinical-trial context and suggests
against probiotics in children with acute infectious gastroenteritis. That does
not conflict with a pediatric **antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention**
recommendation: they are different questions. Where organizations do disagree on
the same question, preserve jurisdiction, publication date, population, endpoint
and certainty rather than collapsing them into a universal positive badge.

Clinical review schema needed before curation:

- Separate exact organism/strain identity, multi-strain formula identity and trial family.
- Record population, indication, prevention/treatment, co-therapy and duration.
- Keep actual tested doses/regimens; justify any interpolation instead of inventing it.
- Distinguish primary patient-important endpoints from secondary, exploratory and surrogate findings.
- Record direction and uncertainty separately from study design/quality.
- State review completeness and search boundaries explicitly, including missing full texts.
- Scope safety separately; failure to prove benefit is not a safety block.

Bl-04 (84 products), Lpc-37 (52), BB536 (41), HN001 (38), DE111 (31), and the rest
of the 36 matched registry IDs remain prioritized full-text work. This focused
review is not a claim that those sources, every PMID in the registry, or every
clinical indication have now been comprehensively verified.
